Cache Replacement Simulator

LRU vs FIFO vs LFU vs Optimal (Bélády)

5,000 accesses × 6 capacities × 4 policies

LRU: Least Recently Used

Uses a hash map (key → node) and a doubly linked list (most recent at the front). On a hit, the node is moved to the front in O(1). On a miss with a full cache, the node at the back is evicted in O(1). Neither structure alone gives O(1) for both lookup and reordering.

FIFO: First In, First Out

A queue: evicts whatever entered first, ignoring how often or how recently it was used. O(1). Can suffer Bélády's anomaly: a bigger cache gives more misses. Try 1 2 3 4 1 2 5 1 2 3 4 5 with capacity 3, then 4.

LFU: Least Frequently Used

A map of frequency → doubly linked list plus a min_freq pointer gives O(1) eviction of the least-used key (ties broken by LRU).

Optimal (Bélády's algorithm)

Evicts the key whose next use is farthest in the future. It needs future knowledge, so it can't be used in practice, but it is the theoretical best and serves as the benchmark.
